Currency Pairs

Learn how currencies are quoted in pairs and understand the difference between major, minor, and exotic currency pairs.

What is a Currency Pair?

In Forex, currencies are traded in pairs. A currency pair shows the value of one currency relative to another currency.

For example, EUR/USD represents the euro compared with the US dollar.

Key Idea

Every Forex trade involves two currencies: the currency being bought and the currency being sold.

Base Currency

The first currency in a currency pair is called the base currency.

In EUR/USD, EUR is the base currency.

Quote Currency

The second currency in a currency pair is called the quote currency.

In EUR/USD, USD is the quote currency.

Example: EUR/USD

EUR = Base Currency
USD = Quote Currency

Major Currency Pairs

Major currency pairs are among the most actively traded currency pairs in the Forex market. They include the US dollar paired with major currencies such as the euro, Japanese yen, British pound, and Swiss franc.

Examples include EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, and USD/CHF.

Minor Currency Pairs

Minor currency pairs, sometimes called cross pairs, are currency pairs that generally do not include the US dollar.

Examples include EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, and GBP/JPY.

Exotic Currency Pairs

Exotic currency pairs generally combine a major currency with the currency of an emerging or smaller economy.

These pairs can have different trading conditions from major pairs, including potentially wider spreads and lower liquidity.

Important

Understanding currency pairs is essential before placing a Forex trade. Always learn how the pair is quoted before interpreting its price movement.

Key Takeaway

A currency pair tells you how much of the quote currency is needed to represent one unit of the base currency.
